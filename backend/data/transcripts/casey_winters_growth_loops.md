# Casey Winters on Retention Loops, Marketplaces, and Sustainable Growth

## Chapter 1: The Truth About Retention vs. Acquisition
Lenny: Casey, you led growth at Pinterest, Grubhub, and Eventbrite. What is the fundamental relationship between retention and acquisition?

Casey Winters: Most startup founders believe acquisition is the hardest part of growth. But acquisition is actually a derivative of retention. If you have great retention, your customers stay around, talk to their friends, write reviews, and generate viral or SEO loops that bring in new users for free. 

If retention is broken, every marketing dollar is wasted because you are trying to fill a bucket with a massive hole at the bottom. At Pinterest, our entire growth strategy was engineered around getting new signups to pin their first 5 items within their first session—because our data proved that was the inflection point for long-term 90-day retention.

## Chapter 2: The Cold Start Problem in Marketplaces
Lenny: How do you solve the cold start problem in two-sided marketplaces like Grubhub or Eventbrite?

Casey Winters: In a two-sided marketplace, you must always constrain the marketplace and solve the supply side first in a hyper-local geography. At Grubhub, we didn't launch nationwide; we launched on three blocks in Chicago around college campuses. We manually signed up 15 pizza places. Once we had high density of supply, demand flowed naturally because diners had instant selection.

## Chapter 3: Feature vs. Product vs. Company
Lenny: How can PMs tell if their new initiative is merely a feature or a venture-scale product?

Casey Winters: A feature solves a single task. A product solves a persistent workflow. A company builds self-reinforcing compound loops across multiple products that create an impenetrable competitive moat.
