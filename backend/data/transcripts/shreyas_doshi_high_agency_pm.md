# Shreyas Doshi on High Agency Product Leadership and The LNO Framework

## Chapter 1: The LNO Framework for Task Prioritization
Lenny: Shreyas, your tweetstorms and frameworks are legendary among PMs. One framework that completely changed how people work is the LNO Framework. Can you break it down?

Shreyas Doshi: Most PMs suffer from chronic burnout because they treat all tasks as requiring equal perfection. The truth is, not all work is created equal. The LNO framework categorizes tasks into three distinct buckets:

1. **L — Leverage Tasks (10x ROI)**: These are the high-stakes, asymmetric bets that change the trajectory of your product or organization. Examples include drafting the core product vision, crafting a winning product strategy doc, establishing key customer discovery insights, or designing high-impact pricing tiers. You should spend 60% of your creative energy here and strive for an **A+ grade**.
2. **N — Neutral Tasks (1x ROI)**: Standard operational duties that keep the train on the tracks. Sprint planning, routine PRD updates, weekly status memos, stakeholder alignment emails. Good enough is good enough. Aim for a solid **B grade** and execute quickly.
3. **O — Overhead Tasks (<0.1x ROI)**: Administrative chores, expense reports, survey responses, boilerplate compliance forms. Do them with minimal effort (a **C or D grade**) or delegate/automate them entirely.

If you try to give every task an A+, you will inevitably give your Leverage tasks a B.

## Chapter 2: High Agency and Radical Ownership
Lenny: What separates a good product manager from a truly exceptional, high-agency product manager?

Shreyas Doshi: High agency is the refusal to accept standard constraints as fixed limitations. When an average PM hits a roadblock—say, "Design is fully booked for two sprints" or "Legal won't approve this API"—they stop and say, "I'm blocked." 

A high-agency PM finds a way. They bend reality. They create low-fidelity Figma prototypes themselves to unblock the engineers, or they sit down directly with Legal and redline the contract together. High-agency leaders do not wait for permission; they operate with radical ownership of the customer outcome.

## Chapter 3: Strategy vs. Execution
Lenny: How should PMs think about balancing strategy and execution?

Shreyas Doshi: Strategy without execution is a daydream. Execution without strategy is a nightmare. A great strategy is a cohesive set of choices that positions your product to win by creating unique value for customers in a way that competitors cannot easily replicate.
