# April Dunford on The 5 Components of B2B Product Positioning

## Chapter 1: What Positioning Actually Means
Lenny: April, you've helped hundreds of tech companies fix their positioning. Why do so many founders get positioning wrong?

April Dunford: Most teams confuse positioning with messaging or taglines. Taglines are the paint on the wall; positioning is the foundation of the house. 

Positioning defines how your product is the absolute best in the world at delivering something that a well-defined set of customers cares deeply about. If you position your product in the wrong market category (e.g., calling an enterprise data warehouse a "business intelligence tool"), customers will compare you against the wrong competitors and evaluate you on features you don't care about.

## Chapter 2: The 5 Interlocking Components of Positioning
Lenny: Walk us through your 5-step positioning framework.

April Dunford:
1. **Competitive Alternatives**: What would customers use if your product didn't exist? (Often Excel, pen and paper, or hiring an intern—not just direct tech competitors).
2. **Unique Differentiated Attributes**: What features or capabilities do you have that the alternatives lack?
3. **Value for Customers**: What business benefit or ROI does that unique attribute unlock? (Features describe *what it does*; Value describes *why the CFO cares*).
4. **Target Customer Segment**: Who cares the most about that unique value? (Your best-fit customers).
5. **Market Category**: What context or framing makes your unique value obvious? (e.g., "The CRM for Real Estate Agents" vs "General CRM").

## Chapter 3: The Danger of the "All-in-One" Trap
Lenny: What is the biggest positioning red flag you see in pitches?

April Dunford: Calling yourself the "All-in-One Platform." When you claim to do everything for everybody, you become the best at nothing for nobody. Pick a sharp category wedge, dominate it, and expand outwards.
