# Rahul Vohra on The Product-Market Fit Engine and The 40% Disappointment Rule

## Chapter 1: The Quantitative Definition of Product-Market Fit
Lenny: Rahul, you built one of the most famous methodologies for quantifying Product-Market Fit (PMF) at Superhuman. How do you measure PMF systematically?

Rahul Vohra: Marc Andreessen famously said you can always feel when PMF is happening because money is piling up and servers are melting. But if you haven't reached PMF yet, that definition is useless because it doesn't give you a metric to optimize toward.

We adopted the Sean Ellis survey question: *"How would you feel if you could no longer use the product?"*
- Very disappointed
- Somewhat disappointed
- Not disappointed

Sean Ellis benchmarked hundreds of startups and found that companies that achieved sustained growth always had **at least 40%** of respondents answer "Very disappointed." When we first surveyed Superhuman users in 2017, our score was only 22%. By building the PMF Engine, we pushed that score from 22% to 58% in under six months.

## Chapter 2: The 4-Step Product-Market Fit Engine
Lenny: Walk us through the four steps of the PMF Engine that took Superhuman from 22% to 58%.

Rahul Vohra:
1. **Segment to find your High-Expectation Customer (HXC)**: Look at the users who answered "Very disappointed." Identify what personas they share (for us, it was founders, executives, VC investors, and managers who lived in email). Filter out feedback from users who answered "Not disappointed" completely—listening to them will lead you into the trap of building bloated features for the wrong audience.
2. **Analyze Feedback to Understand Love vs. Holdouts**: Ask the "Somewhat disappointed" users who match your HXC profile: *"What is the main benefit you receive from the product?"* and *"What is holding you back from loving it?"*
3. **Double Down on What Users Love (50% Roadmap)**: Dedicate 50% of your engineering sprint capacity to making your core superpowers even faster and better (e.g., keyboard shortcuts, instant search, split inboxes).
4. **Address the Roadblocks for the Somewhat Disappointed Users (50% Roadmap)**: Dedicate the other 50% of your sprint capacity to fixing the top 2-3 objections cited by the "Somewhat disappointed" HXC cohort (e.g., mobile apps, calendar integration).

## Chapter 3: Speed as a Feature and Keyboard-First Design
Lenny: Superhuman made speed a fundamental product differentiator. Why is speed so transformative for engagement?

Rahul Vohra: When an interface responds in under 100 milliseconds, the human brain perceives it as instantaneous and effortless. Speed creates cognitive flow. Every 100ms of lag introduces subtle micro-frustrations that degrade user joy.
