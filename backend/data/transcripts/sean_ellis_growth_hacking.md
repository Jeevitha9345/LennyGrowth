# Sean Ellis on Growth Hacking, The ICE Scoring Framework, and The 40% PMF Survey

## Chapter 1: The Origin of the 40% Product-Market Fit Rule
Lenny: Sean, you invented the famous "40% disappointment" survey question that everyone from Superhuman to Dropbox uses. Where did it come from?

Sean Ellis: When I joined early startups like LogMeIn and Dropbox, founders would ask me to run growth marketing immediately. But I learned the hard way that running growth experiments on a product without product-market fit is suicide. 

So I created a simple diagnostic question: *"How would you feel if you could no longer use this product tomorrow?"*
- Very disappointed
- Somewhat disappointed
- Not disappointed

After benchmarking more than 100 startups, a clear pattern emerged: products that achieved breakout, self-sustaining growth scored **>= 40% in 'Very disappointed'**. Companies below 40% always struggled with churn and high CAC.

## Chapter 2: The ICE Prioritization Framework (Impact, Confidence, Ease)
Lenny: Growth teams generate hundreds of ideas every month. How should teams prioritize which growth experiments to run first?

Sean Ellis: We use the **ICE Framework** to rank hypotheses on a scale from 1 to 10:
1. **Impact (1–10)**: If this experiment succeeds, how massive will the growth lift be?
2. **Confidence (1–10)**: How certain are we that this will work based on prior user research or data?
3. **Ease (1–10)**: How fast and cheap is this to build and test? (A 10 is an hour of work; a 1 requires a 2-month backend rewrite).

**ICE Score = (Impact + Confidence + Ease) / 3**

Run the highest-scoring tests in weekly growth sprints. High experimentation velocity (3–5 tests/week) is the single biggest predictor of growth success.

## Chapter 3: The Famous Dropbox Viral Referral Program
Lenny: You also helped design Dropbox's legendary two-sided referral program (giving 500MB free storage to both inviter and invitee). What made it work so well?

Sean Ellis: Double-sided incentives that directly tie into the product's core value proposition. Giving free storage rewarded users with the exact utility they wanted, directly fueling retention while creating a 60% viral expansion loop.
