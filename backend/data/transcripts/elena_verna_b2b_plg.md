# Elena Verna on B2B Product-Led Growth and Modern Monetization Loops

## Chapter 1: The Core Definition of B2B Product-Led Growth (PLG)
Lenny: Elena, you've advised dozens of iconic B2B companies like Miro, Amplitude, and Netlify on growth. How do you define Product-Led Growth today, and how is it different from traditional sales-led motions?

Elena Verna: PLG is not just about having a free trial or a freemium tier. PLG is an organizational business methodology where product usage serves as the primary driver of acquisition, retention, and expansion. In a sales-led model, you convince someone to buy before they ever experience value. In a product-led model, you deliver value first, and you monetize second. The product becomes your primary storefront, salesperson, and customer success manager.

If you don't have end-user love and rapid time-to-value (TTV) within the first five minutes of onboarding, no amount of sales outreach will make you truly product-led. You have to design the product so that an individual can sign up with their work email, get immediate utility, and naturally invite their teammates.

## Chapter 2: Freemium vs. Free Trial vs. Reverse Trials
Lenny: How should founders and growth PMs decide between a pure freemium model, a time-limited free trial, and what you call the "reverse trial"?

Elena Verna: This is where most early-stage teams make costly mistakes. 
1. **Pure Freemium**: Great for products with wide horizontal appeal and strong network effects (like Miro or Figma). You keep the free tier forever, but you gate on usage limits, team collaboration seats, or advanced administrative features.
2. **Time-Limited Free Trial (e.g., 14 days)**: Effective when your product delivers deep value quickly, but has high infrastructure costs per user. The downside is that 14 days is often not enough for enterprise buying committees to finalize procurement.
3. **The Reverse Trial (Elena's Favorite)**: You give new users access to the highest-tier enterprise plan for 14 days. When the trial ends, instead of locking them out and breaking their workflow, you gracefully downgrade them to a generous freemium tier. This hooks them on premium features (like SAML SSO, advanced analytics, or unlimited boards), while preserving the organic user habit on the free plan.

## Chapter 3: Product-Led Sales (PLS) and Product-Qualified Leads (PQLs)
Lenny: At what point should a PLG company layer on a sales team, and how do you define a Product-Qualified Lead (PQL)?

Elena Verna: PLG and Sales are not enemies; PLG is the top-of-funnel engine that feeds your sales team high-intent opportunities. You layer on sales when you see "hand-raisers" and organic enterprise clustering. 

A Product-Qualified Lead (PQL) is an account that has achieved two specific criteria:
1. **Value Threshold**: They have reached the core activation milestone (e.g., created 3 boards and shared them with 5 coworkers).
2. **Buying Signal**: They hit an account-level limit or exhibit cross-departmental adoption.

Your sales reps should never cold-call accounts that haven't touched the product. Instead, they reach out as consultative advisors to help consolidate 10 separate team subscriptions into an enterprise-wide agreement.

## Chapter 4: Monetization Loops and Retention as the Foundation
Lenny: What is the most critical metric that growth teams neglect when trying to scale revenue?

Elena Verna: Retention! Growth is a leaky bucket if retention is broken. You cannot grow on top of poor retention. Before you spend a single dollar on paid acquisition or aggressive paywalls, measure your cohort retention curves. 

Your retention curve must flatten parallel to the x-axis. If it heads toward zero, you do not have product-market fit. Once retention is stable, build natural monetization loops where the more value a team extracts from the product, the more seats or usage credits they naturally consume.
